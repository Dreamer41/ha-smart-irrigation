# ZoneFlow 1.8.0 — A water estimate that follows the plant and the weather

**What's new**

- **FAO-56 water estimate.** A new **Water Demand Model**, **FAO-56
  (Penman-Monteith)**, the standard method for how much water a plant uses. It
  works from the temperature alone and gets more accurate with each sensor you
  add: outdoor humidity, wind speed and solar radiation (new optional fields in
  the zone and area settings), or the humidity and wind of your weather entity.
  An input without a sensor falls back on a site setting (wind at this spot,
  humid or dry climate, by the sea). Each day is summed from that day's real
  readings; a sensor counts for a day only when its readings cover at least 18
  hours. The weekly target is the average daily water use since the last
  watering, less the rain credit as before.
  - The **Why?** panel says where each input came from: your sensor, the weather
    service, the zone's setting, or estimated from the temperature.
  - FAO-56 usually gives less water in humid weather and more on dry, windy days.
  - Greenhouse and indoor zones use their inside humidity and light sensors.
- **A crop curve instead of one fixed crop factor.** The new **Crop Curve**
  select picks a crop (52 are included) and the zone waters by that crop's own
  season: little for a young plant, the most at mid-season, less late. Annual
  crops count from the planting date (until it is set, the Crop Factor slider is
  used and Check my setup says so); perennials follow the year from green-up.
  - **Crop Curve Strength** (50-150 %) tunes the curve for your own plant, and
    the **Crop Factor Today** sensor shows the number and where it comes from.
  - **Crop Factor Follows Climate** (a switch, on by default for a picked curve)
    raises the curve in a dry, windy week and lowers it in a calm, humid one.
  - **Sun Exposure** says how much sun and reflected heat the spot gets: shade
    all day, morning sun, afternoon sun, full sun (the default) or reflected heat
    against a wall or paving.
  - A curve that counts from planting also covers the young plant's growth, so
    the growth ramp is not applied on top of it.
- **Root-zone limit.** Four new settings: **Root Depth**, **Soil Water-Holding
  Capacity**, **Allowed Soil Drying** and **Wetted Share of the Zone**. With Root
  Depth set, a watering or a deep soak never gives more than the roots can hold,
  and the days between waterings shorten when one interval would dry the soil too
  far, for example a drip bed on light soil in a hot week. It can only make
  waterings sooner and smaller. The **Root Zone Holds** sensor and the Why? panel
  show what it holds and what it changed.
- **Weather hold: frost, rain and wind.** The frost wait is now one hold with a
  reason. A due watering waits while the weather is against it, checks again
  every hour for up to six hours, then waits for its next scheduled time. One
  phone message per cycle and reason; a restart picks the wait up again. A press
  of **Run Now** is never held by rain or wind, only by frost.
  - **Rain sensor.** A zone or area can name a wet/dry sensor. While it reads
    wet, due watering waits. A sensor that has read wet for a day without a change
    is treated as stuck: ignored, shown in Check my setup, and a Repairs notice
    asks you to look at it.
  - **Rain Sensor Rain Rate** (0 = off). A zone with no rain gauge and no Weather
    Underground can count the sensor's wet time as rain, at most 30 mm a day. Why?
    says the rain is an estimate.
  - **Wind Limit** for sprinkler and micro-sprinkler zones: a due watering waits
    while the wind is above it. Drip and soaker zones never wait for wind.
- **Master valve.** A zone can name a backup shut-off valve upstream, for example
  after a pump shared by several zones. It opens before a watering's first pulse
  and closes after the last one. It also closes for safety, and stays closed until
  **Reset Lock**, when a zone valve does not confirm it closed, when the
  stuck-valve watchdog fires, or when the flow meter keeps counting or the pump
  keeps drawing power while every zone valve behind it is closed. A phone alert
  goes out even with notifications off.
  - It can be **normally closed** (the default and the recommendation: it shuts by
    itself in a power cut) or **normally open** (a relay switched on closes it).
  - With a flow meter or a pump power sensor, ZoneFlow tests it once a month at the
    end of a routine watering. It closes the master, opens the zone valve for half a
    minute and checks that the water and the pump stop. A failed test sends a phone
    alert and is not repeated for a month: press **Master Valve Checked** after you
    have fixed or checked the valve, and the next routine watering tests it again.
- **Watering by the flow meter.** A new select, **Watering Stops On**: **Planned
  time** (the default, as before) or **Flow meter**. With the flow meter, each
  pulse ends when its share of the litres has gone through, and a short pulse is
  made up by the later ones. The planned time plus **Flow Mode Time Allowance**
  (50 %) is the limit, never past the runtime cap. **Zone Area** turns
  millimetres into litres.
  - Far too much water (a burst or loose pipe) closes the valve, and the master
    valve behind it, with an alert even with notifications off. A meter that stops
    counting finishes the watering on time with one warning. A run that can't give
    its litres in time warns of low flow.
  - Zones sharing one meter across different pumps water by time.
- **Valve overlap also works when zones share one flow meter.** The second or two
  of shared water is counted in both zones' litres, which is too little to matter.
- **Wind, humidity and sunshine from nearby Weather Underground stations.** A new
  option, **Also borrow wind, humidity and sunshine** (off by default), gives the
  median of the chosen stations to every outdoor zone that has no sensor and no
  weather entity for that reading.
- **Valve entities.** A zone's valve can now be a `valve.*` entity as well as a
  `switch.*`.

**What's fixed**

- **A flow meter that reports a rate (L/min, m³/h, gal/min) is read as a rate.**
  It was read as a running total, so its litres per run, the weekly litres and the
  No Flow Detected warning were wrong. A meter whose unit is neither a volume nor a
  rate (for example "pulses") is no longer read as litres; Check my setup says so.
- **Sunrise and sunset schedules now start the watering.** A zone whose routine or
  deep soak was set to before/after sunrise or sunset never watered on schedule on
  current Home Assistant.
- **The stuck-valve alarm goes out even if the close command fails.**
- **A valve that is offline is no longer taken as closed.** Its lock stays until
  the power-loss or stale-lock watchdog releases it.
- **Copy settings and the Deep Soak switch no longer stop a watering in
  progress.** While a zone waters they are refused with "zone busy".
- **A restart across 23:59:50 no longer loses a day.**
- **Fewer disk writes.**

## What changes for existing zones

Your zones should water the way they did before you update, unless you choose
otherwise. In detail:

- **Water demand model.** A zone that already exists **keeps its model**
  (temperature tiers or the ET curve). Only **new** zones start on FAO-56. After
  two weeks of FAO-56 days on record, an existing zone gets a Repairs item offering
  the switch, with both weekly amounts side by side. Nothing switches without your
  yes.
- **If you switch a zone to FAO-56**, or pick a crop curve, its run times change.
  FAO-56 usually gives less water in humid weather and more on dry, windy days; a
  crop curve follows the crop's season instead of one fixed number. Watch the
  plants for a week or two, as after any change (the Why? panel shows the numbers).
- **Crop factor.** Existing zones keep the fixed **Crop Factor** and the Crop Curve
  select starts on *Fixed*. Sun Exposure starts on full sun (a factor of 1.00), so
  it changes nothing until you set it.
- **Root-zone limit.** Existing zones keep **Root Depth at 0**, which is off. They
  water exactly as before. Check my setup suggests setting it.
- **Wind limit.** **Off (0) for sprinkler and micro-sprinkler zones that already
  exist.** Set it yourself if you want it. Only new sprinkler zones start at
  20 km/h (micro-sprinklers 25).
- **New defaults are for new zones only.** The values a new zone is seeded with
  (the root depth of its crop, the holding capacity of its soil, the wetted share
  of its irrigation method, the wind limit, the curve of its plant type) are not
  applied to zones you already have.
- **Rain sensor, master valve, Watering Stops On, Weather Underground borrowing:**
  all off or unset until you set them. Watering still stops on the planned time.
- **Flow meters that report a rate.** If one of your zones points at a meter that
  reports L/min, m³/h or gal/min, its litres per run and weekly litres will now be
  right; earlier figures were not. Totals already recorded are not recalculated.

**Checked before release:** a 24-hour sandbox soak and seven extra test
instances (upgrade from saved 1.7.10 data, all-imperial, bare minimum with no
sensors, sensor faults and zones removed while running, 60 zones, polar-night
sun schedules, a clock running ten times fast), a code review, an outside bug
hunt (its real findings are fixed), the upgrade tests from 1.7.10 and the full
test suite. Rain Today and the rain estimate can lag a rain sensor by up to ten
minutes (they are counted on the ten-minute heartbeat as well as on changes).
Not checked on real hardware: a pulse flow meter that holds its rate, a motorised
`valve` entity and a normally-open master valve.

## Update your dashboard

A hand-made dashboard can add the new entities for a zone, for example in an
Entities card (Edit dashboard → the card → Show code editor):

```yaml
type: entities
title: Water estimate
entities:
  - entity: select.<zone>_water_demand_model
  - entity: select.<zone>_crop_curve
  - entity: number.<zone>_crop_curve_strength
  - entity: sensor.<zone>_crop_factor_today
  - entity: switch.<zone>_crop_factor_follows_climate
  - entity: select.<zone>_sun_exposure
  - entity: sensor.<zone>_root_zone_holds
  - entity: select.<zone>_watering_stops_on
```

Replace `<zone>` with the zone's entity prefix. New number entities: Root Depth,
Soil Water-Holding Capacity, Allowed Soil Drying, Wetted Share of the Zone, Zone
Area, Flow Mode Time Allowance, Wind Limit (sprinkler zones only) and Rain Sensor
Rain Rate. The ZoneFlow card shows them by itself, under the zone's **Settings**; some appear
only when they apply (for example Wind Limit only on sprinkler zones). Shortcut: paste `AI_SETUP.md` into an AI
assistant and ask it to update the dashboard for this version.

## After updating

Restart Home Assistant. Existing zones keep their saved state and nothing resets.
To use the new features on a zone you already have, set them under the zone's
**Configure** (sensors, master valve, rain sensor) and on its device page (the
selects and sliders above). Run **Check my setup** on each zone afterwards.
